# Style variation and authorship preservation

Read this when the user asks for a particular tone, a less generic or less AI-like script, several stylistic variants, adaptation of an existing voice, or preservation of their own writing habits.

## Primary principle

Distinctive writing comes from a coherent dramatic point of view, not a pile of unusual words. Select only the style decisions that serve the current film, keep them stable enough to form a voice, and allow local variation under pressure.

Never claim that these methods guarantee human attribution or evade detectors. Do not add errors, random slang, gratuitous nonlinear structure, loose ends, brands, metaphors, or quirks as camouflage.

## Style matrix

Choose a small number of axes and state where the project sits. Do not maximize every axis.

| Axis | Range |
|---|---|
| Reality mode | observational realism ↔ heightened theatricality |
| Action-line voice | dry/objective ↔ lyrical/subjective |
| Visual density | spare negative space ↔ tactile production detail |
| Dialogue load | silence/action-led ↔ dialogue-driven |
| Dialogue compression | blunt/direct ↔ layered/indirect |
| Emotional rendering | restrained behavior ↔ operatic display |
| Time | linear ↔ braided or disclosure-driven |
| Rhythm | slow observational ↔ compressed kinetic |
| Humor | warm ↔ dry ↔ caustic ↔ absurd |
| Violence/intimacy distance | withheld ↔ procedural ↔ immediate |
| Sound | naturalistic background ↔ strong subjective motif |
| Transitions | hard causal cuts ↔ associative image/sound bridges |
| Production scale | contained ↔ expansive |

The `Sound` axis records how sound is used. Whether non-diegetic music exists in this film at all is a separate settlement made with the sound contract, not a position on this range.

For each axis you commit to, also name the nearest register the work will be mistaken for, and the one observable difference that separates them. The position on the range tells a reviewer where the project sits; the excluded neighbor tells them when a page has slipped.

Do the same for the genre promise: name the adjacent genre whose pleasures this film must not deliver, and state what a reader would see on the page if it had started delivering them.

Keep every exclusion observable — a difference visible in scene entry, in information order, in what would be in front of the camera, in how long a beat is held, in what a character is permitted to say, or in how an emotion is allowed to arrive. An exclusion phrased as a mood cannot be checked and will not survive a rewrite. Two or three of these are enough; a full inventory of everything the film is not becomes its own house style.

Add project-specific axes only when they change actual pages.

## Voice profile from samples

Use samples only when the user owns them, supplied them for this task, they are licensed, or they are public domain.

Extract abstract features:

- action-block length and sentence rhythm;
- verb strength and description density;
- visual subjectivity and metaphor tolerance;
- use of white space, fragments, capitalization, transitions, and parentheticals;
- dialogue compression, interruption, silence, humor, profanity, and formality;
- scene entry/exit habits and sequence rhythm;
- how emotion, violence, intimacy, and exposition are handled;
- recurring strengths, liabilities, and deliberate exceptions.

Do not store or reproduce long source passages. Do not copy sentences, signature dialogue, characters, scene construction, plot order, or uniquely identifying expressions. A style profile is a feature summary, not a phrase bank.

## Requests to imitate a creator

- For a living writer or filmmaker, do not closely imitate their signature style. Translate the request into high-level axes, traditions, genres, techniques, and constraints, then produce an original combination.
- For a deceased or public-domain source, still avoid copying recognizable lines, scenes, characters, or plot order unless the user explicitly requests a licensed adaptation.
- When the user gives several references, assign each a different job—such as dialogue pressure, temporal structure, visual restraint, or humor—rather than averaging all surfaces into an imitation soup.
- Preserve cultural specificity supplied by the user; do not substitute a generic Hollywood register.

## Variation that matters

When asked for variants, change dramatic choices, not just diction. Useful variant dimensions include:

- conflict engine;
- who holds leverage and when it changes;
- audience knowledge and reveal order;
- objective, tactic, or moral cost;
- scene entry point and exit image;
- silence-to-dialogue ratio;
- spatial use and object interaction;
- pace and pressure curve;
- visual or sound strategy;
- ending mode.

Hold the user's locked facts, character identities, required event, and delivery constraints fixed. Explain the true variable between variants.

## Anti-template diagnostics

Apply only to observed problems:

- **Theme over-explanation:** narrator/action lines or climactic dialogue state what events mean. Keep the evidence; remove the answer key.
- **Generic turn:** the helpful stranger, scheduled confession, convenient evidence, or twist would recur in most versions of the premise. Replace it with a consequence only these characters and conditions could produce.
- **Uniform middle:** adjacent scenes share the same length, mode, pressure, and reveal pattern. Change scene engine or information geometry.
- **Tidy causality:** every setup pays off on schedule and every beat exists only to service one line. Preserve economy while allowing texture, competing obligations, and consequences from outside the protagonist's plan.
- **Performed emotion:** every feeling becomes breath, pulse, clenched hands, weather, or metaphor. Use behavior, choices, plain speech, silence, and embodied reaction selectively.
- **Tidy acceptance ending:** the protagonist understands, forgives, explains the theme, and resolves every relationship. Keep this only when the whole film earns it.
- **Inventory writing:** repeated groups of three, repeated action-block lengths, every scene ending on a reveal, every line carrying polished subtext.
- **Generic specificity:** invented brands, cultural details, jobs, or lived experience used only to appear human. Verify or remove.

Do not reverse each suspected AI tendency into a compulsory human marker. Aggregate prose statistics are not causal screenplay rules.

## Preserve the author's actual voice

Priority order:

1. safety, rights, and delivery constraints;
2. the user's current request and locked material;
3. the current film's approved voice profile and story facts;
4. explicitly confirmed long-term author preferences;
5. reference-derived craft features;
6. generic defaults.

Do not infer a permanent preference from one correction. Do not normalize a mannerism the user's own samples consistently use. Quotes and intentionally stylized in-world text are load-bearing unless the user asks to change them.

## Calibration and slack

- Select a few meaningful choices; do not accumulate every anti-template technique.
- Allow plain functional lines and connective action. Distinctiveness needs contrast.
- Keep some scenes more conventional when convention makes them clearer or more producible.
- A quiet scene, establishing scene, monologue, philosophical exchange, linear structure, explicit emotion, or resolved ending is not inherently AI-like. Diagnose frequency, function, and execution.
- Overcorrection has its own fingerprint: forced obscurity, constant asymmetry, arbitrary discontinuity, universal terseness, and relentless originality make the screenplay feel engineered.

## Authorship quality gates

- Could the same pages belong to a different premise after names and nouns are changed? If yes, specificity is insufficient.
- Do the selected style axes remain recognizable across scenes without forcing identical syntax?
- Can major characters be distinguished through tactics, relationships, and information behavior?
- Do variants change scene logic as well as word choice?
- Did the rewrite preserve the user's strongest original decisions, including rough or ordinary ones that serve the film?
- Is every invented real-world detail accurate or explicitly marked for research?
